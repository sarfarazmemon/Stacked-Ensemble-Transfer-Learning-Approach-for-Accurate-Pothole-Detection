Potholes are among the most common forms of road damage and can significantly affect vehicle stability and driving safety. Traditional road inspection techniques are time-consuming and not suitable for widespread monitoring. Early detection of potholes is critical to maintaining road quality and avoiding accidents. In this study, two sophisticated pretrained deep learning models  Xception and EfficientNetB0 are combined in Stacked Ensemble Transfer Learning (SETL_Pothole) framework for automated pothole detection in road images thereby increasing prediction accuracy, reducing overfitting, and improving overall robustness. Additionally, data augmentation techniques are used to overcome underfitting and increase the diversity of the training samples. In this study, we used two datasets to evaluate the effectiveness and reliability of the recommended approach. First, we used a custom Road Anomaly Detection (RAD) dataset to select relevant frames from road videos. Second, we used a publicly available dataset on Mendeley. We compared the proposed SETL_Pothole model with other ensemble methods such as Bagging Ensemble Transfer Learning (BETL) and Voting Ensemble Transfer Learning (VETL). The experimental results show that the SETL approach achieves the highest accuracy (98.80%), outperforming VETL (94.50%) and BETL (92.81%). This SETL_Potholes model successfully combines the strengths of several deep learning models, providing a highly precise, reliable, and effective approach for monitoring road conditions and identifying potholes.
